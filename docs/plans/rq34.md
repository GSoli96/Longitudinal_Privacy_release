# Sub-plan for RQ3 and RQ4 (DRAFT, to be approved by the authors)

Status: draft of 29 October 2026 for `docs/plans/rq34.md`. Nothing has been run. It derives from `docs/specs/2026-10-20-rerun-design.md` §6, §7.2 and §10b.2-3, from the plan of 23 October (cloud models) and from the plan of 29 October (local compute, no spending). Where a datum is not in the files I write "to be decided" and list it among the open questions.

## 1. Objective and hypotheses

RQ3: are the inferred and validated attributes enough to generate realistic, personalised phishing messages? RQ4: how easily do automatic classifiers recognise the generated messages, compared with public benchmarks, and what follows for defence?

Working hypotheses for RQ3 (to be tested, not expected as results). A message built with inferred attributes receives higher personalisation scores than one without personal attributes and than one with explicit facts only; the no-documents control (role and type of organisation only) ranks below the variant with documents. The current paper leaves this comparison to future work, and specification §7.2 introduces it.

Hypotheses for RQ4. In the current paper, detectors trained on the generated corpus reach an F1 of 1.000 with zero variance; the specification attributes the suspicion to style shortcuts (negatives of undocumented origin, fixed phrases, signatures, placeholders). With negatives generated by the same model for the same person, separability is expected to drop. The relation between personalisation and detectability is open: detectors may remain effective because the register stays uniform, or lose recall on the personalised variant. The plan measures both and does not fix a direction.

## 2. Input data

Profiles: attributes validated by majority vote, plausible, coherent and realistic, at most 12, taken from the 100% step (§10b.3). One filter for all cases; the stricter filters of Cases C and F in the current paper are not kept (the specification does not provide for them).

Cases: A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, only those whose judging is complete at the time of generation. Persons per case, from the files and the current paper, to be reconfirmed after the rerun: A 288 (ambiguity with 250 open), B 248, C 95, D 158 (159 in the paper), E 265, F 16 or 18, G 80, H 467; about 1,620 persons. Status on 29 October: B and A partly still to be judged, H to be loaded, D to be redone.

For each person, three message variants (no personal attributes, explicit only, explicit plus inferred), each in the two types BEC and spear phishing: six positive messages per person with the primary generator. The second generator (section 3) works on the same set of persons, but the number of persons is to be decided (question 3). Negatives: legitimate institutional messages generated for the same person, with the same name and the same attributes (§10b.3), in a number equal to the positives for each variant (to be confirmed, question 4).

Sample: the specification provides for all persons in the RQ3-RQ4 core. If the local cost does not allow it, it is reduced per case by stratified sampling with a fixed seed; the threshold is to be decided after measuring the speed (section 7).

Public benchmarks for training and comparison: CEAS_08, Enron, Ling, Nazario, Nigerian Fraud, SpamAssassin, run once and reused for every case. They are in English, whereas the generated messages are in Italian: a limitation to declare (§6). The 1,570 legitimate Italian e-mails of the current paper have no documented provenance (comment D35 in the `.tex`): they are not reused as negatives until the source is clarified.

## 3. Generators

Primary: the attacker `gpt-oss:20b`, in the local version (`gpt-oss:20b-local`, with a digest different from the cloud one; both to be reported) for the cases redone locally, and the cloud caches for the cases already produced. The plan of 23 October provided for generation in the cloud; the quota is stalled (1 key out of 32, reactivation estimated 1-2 November) and the no-spending rule applies, so new generation is local. This PC sustains 4-7 tokens per second with `gpt-oss:20b`, which is too slow; generation goes on the Alienware after the judging of B, with the speed to be measured.

Second generator, of a different family from the primary, neither judge nor detector (§10b.2). Local candidates already present in the files and outside the gpt-oss family: the models of the D99 annotation (`llama3.1:8b`, `phi4-mini`, `deepseek-r1:7b`) and `qwen3.5:9b`, which however is a local judge and therefore excluded. A reasonable proposal is `llama3.1:8b`; the choice belongs to the authors and must be recorded in the log (question 1). Models that do not fit in 32 GB (the 120b) are excluded.

Generation options: the current code (`src/phishing.py`) uses temperature 0.0; the rerun uses temperature 1.0 for the attacker and the cache as a record, because identical calls give different texts. For the messages the choice is to be decided (question 5). The BEC and spear phishing prompts are reused verbatim as part of the method, with the organisation name and the domain taken from the configuration.

## 4. Detectors

Family A, classical on TF-IDF: the ten pipelines of `src/detection.py` (logistic regression, two Naive Bayes, Linear SVC, SGD, tree, Random Forest, Extra Trees, Gradient Boosting, AdaBoost). Computed on CPU, no cost. A variant with textual style features only (length, punctuation, presence of URLs and urgency phrases) is added to separate style from content.

Family B, multilingual and Italian Transformers (six names in `NLP_MODEL_NAMES`): local training, requires PyTorch with CUDA and the explicit approval of the author (plan of 23 October). In the plan of 29 October, the only item listed among this PC's tasks is "classical RQ4 detectors"; the Transformers remain to be decided (question 6).

Family C, a local zero-shot and few-shot LLM detector with the prompts of `src/detection.py` (answer 0 or 1), of a different family from the two generators; if it coincides with a judge, this is declared. Candidate: to be decided (question 2). Uninterpretable answers or failed calls are counted separately and never become a label (§6); the current parser `parse_binary_label` returns 0 if it finds no digit, so it must be changed.

Evaluation settings. Main: training on the public benchmarks only, test on the generated corpus. Secondary: training and test on the generated corpus with a split by person (no person in both); split by case (train on some cases, test on others); split by generator (train on the primary, test on the second and vice versa). Ensemble members are chosen without looking at the test set.

Metrics: AUC ROC, AUPRC, F1 and recall at 1% false positives, with person-cluster bootstrap intervals. Realistic proportions of 1:10 and 1:100 obtained by reweighting or by subsampling the positives. Warning: with few negatives per case, 1% false positives has a resolution of a single example, so recall at fixed FPR is computed on the pool of cases and declared. Complete messages, without truncation.

## 5. Controls

Non-personalised baseline: variant without personal attributes, same generator, same persons. No-documents control: the model receives only role and type of organisation (§7.5), same judges and same rubric. Artefact controls on the text: removal of the organisation name, fixed urgency phrases, signatures and placeholders; length-only and style-only baselines; list of the features with the highest weight. Alarm: an F1 value equal to or above 0.99 stops the stage (`ALARM_DETECTION_F1`); on the generated corpus this already happened, so it must be established on which settings the alarm applies (question 7). Personalisation rubric: LLM panel plus human check on a sample (§7.2); the judges are those of the rerun, hence outside the attacker's family but not outside that of the second generator still to be chosen, to be verified.

## 6. Ethical aspects

No message is sent, neither to real persons nor to test mailboxes. The messages serve only for the offline evaluation of the detectors. The generated texts contain personal data (name and attributes) and stay in `data/private/`, never in tracked files, in chat or in messages to workers. In the released version the links use `.example` hosts, and whether to publish the corpus is open (`docs/ANONYMISATION.md`, §6.4 and question 7 of that file). Cases F and G remain in tier 2 because of their small cohorts. Past cloud runs sent texts to an external service: this is to be declared in Ethics and to the DPO; the new runs are local. The paper already reports that no message was sent (Ethical Considerations section).

## 7. Compute cost

Number of generations: persons × 2 types × 3 variants for the positives, plus the negatives (question 4), for each generator. With about 1,620 persons that is about 9,700 positives per generator before the negatives; the figure depends on the actual persons. Each body is 160-240 words from the prompt; the tokens per message and the speed of `gpt-oss:20b-local` and of the second generator on the 5090 are not in the files: to be measured with a pilot of a few dozen calls before fixing the sample. Classical detectors and style features: CPU, minutes per case. Local LLM detector: one call per message and per setting, cost to be estimated with the same pilot. Transformers: to be estimated only if approved.

## 8. Code steps, in order

1. `rerun/phish_select.py` and `tests/rerun/test_phish_select.py`: selection of attributes from the majority vote (100% step, at most 12, one filter for all), counters of discards.
2. `rerun/phish_gen.py` and tests: generation of the three variants and of the negatives per person, cache by hash, seed, retry and count of invalid JSON, model chosen by configuration; extraction of explicit facts only with a separate call (§10b.3).
3. `rerun/phish_corpus.py` and tests: assembly of the corpus with person identifier, case, variant, generator; check that all input persons are present in the output.
4. `rerun/benchmarks.py` and tests: loading of the six benchmarks, cleaning, hash and version; single run with reuse.
5. `rerun/detect_classic.py` and tests: ten classifiers with splits by person, by case and by generator; length-only and style-only variants; highest-weight features.
6. `rerun/detect_metrics.py` and tests on toy data with known outcome: AUC, AUPRC, F1, recall at FPR 1%, proportions 1:10 and 1:100, bootstrap per person (reuses `rerun/stats.py`).
7. `rerun/detect_llm.py` and tests: local LLM detector with zero-shot and few-shot prompts, separate count of errors, no mapping to a label.
8. `rerun/artefacts.py` and tests: removal of organisation, phrases, signatures and placeholders; rerun of the detectors on the cleaned text.
9. `rerun/rubric.py` and tests: personalisation rubric and sample for the human check.
10. Connect the stages to `rerun/run_case.py` and `rerun/progress.py`, with alarms and summaries in `results/rerun/` (`SUMMARY_RQ3.md`, `SUMMARY_RQ4.md`, LaTeX tables).
11. After approval: Transformers in `rerun/detect_transformer.py`, only if approved.

State of the existing code: `src/phishing.py` has the prompts, the selection and `generate_message`; `src/detection.py` has the ten models, the basic metrics, the LLM prompts and the Transformer names, but not the training loop; in `rerun/` there is not yet any stage for RQ3 or RQ4. `src/` did not produce the rerun numbers and needs revision for the common configuration.
